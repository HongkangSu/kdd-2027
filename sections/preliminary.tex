\section{Preliminaries}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

We study mixed-autonomy dynamic routing: connected and autonomous vehicles
(CAVs) and human-driven vehicles (HDVs) travel over a directed road network
under time-varying departure demand, and a controller routes the CAVs.

\noindent\textbf{Definition 1 (Segment-Node Road Network).}
The network is a directed graph \(G=(N,E)\) over road segments: each node
\(u\in N\) is a segment with static attributes (length, speed limit, lane
count, capacity, free-flow travel time), and an edge \((u,v)\in E\) allows
direct movement from \(u\) to \(v\).  \(A\) denotes the adjacency matrix
under a fixed node ordering.

\noindent\textbf{Definition 2 (Vehicles).}
Epochs \(t=0,\ldots,T\) partition the departure horizon, with active
population
\(\mathcal{N}_t=\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{CAV}}_t\cup\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{HDV}}_t\)
at epoch \(t\).  Vehicle \(i\) has an origin \(o_i\), a destination \(g_i\),
a departure time \(\tau_i^{\mathrm{dep}}\), and a type in
\(\{\mathrm{CAV},\mathrm{HDV}\}\); \(u_{i,t}\) is its current segment.
CAVs follow the routing policy; HDVs cannot be rerouted.

\noindent\textbf{Definition 3 (Route Candidates and Decision Timing).}
An active CAV \(i\) at epoch \(t\) selects one route from
\(\mathcal{P}_{i,t}=\{P^1_{i,t},\ldots,P^{K}_{i,t}\}\), where each candidate
is a segment sequence from \(u_{i,t}\) to \(g_i\), generated by repeated
Dijkstra search with a diversity penalty
(Appendix~\ref{app:math-details}), and the action is the route index
\(a_{i,t}\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\).  An HDV chooses its route only at its
departure time \(\tau_i^{\mathrm{dep}}\).

\subsection{Markov Decision Process}

The process is modeled as an MDP
\(\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{T},r,\gamma)\) over the
global state.  Each CAV acts from its own observation, so execution is
partially observable, and learning follows centralized training with
decentralized execution.

\noindent\textbf{Definition 4 (Observation/State).}
The global state \(s_t\in\mathcal{S}\) contains the road graph, the
scheduled vehicles with their attributes, and the current route candidates.
Each active CAV observes \(o_{i,t}=\mathcal{O}_i(s_t)\): its position,
destination, candidate routes, and route-level traffic summaries.

\noindent\textbf{Definition 5 (Action).}
With \(\mathcal{I}_t=\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{CAV}}_t\) the active CAVs at
epoch \(t\), the joint action is
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{rcl}
a_t&=&(a_{i,t})_{i\in\mathcal{I}_t},
\end{array}
\end{equation}
where \(a_{i,t}=k\) selects candidate route \(P^k_{i,t}\).  HDV route
choices are generated by background human-routing behavior and are part of
the environment.

\noindent\textbf{Definition 6 (Transition).}
Given the departure demand \(D_t\) of newly departing vehicles and the HDV
traffic state \(H_t^{\mathrm{HDV}}\) induced by their own route choices,
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{l}
s_{t+1}\sim
\mathcal{T}(\cdot\mid s_t,a_t,D_t,H_t^{\mathrm{HDV}})
\end{array}
\end{equation}
injects departing vehicles, applies the selected routes, advances all
vehicles, and updates segment speeds, occupancies, and active sets.

\noindent\textbf{Definition 7 (Objective/Reward).}
Each completed vehicle \(i\) has travel time
\(\Delta_i=t_i^{\mathrm{arr}}-\tau_i^{\mathrm{dep}}\), and the objective is
the expected global mean travel time
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{rcl}
\min\quad
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{all}}&=&
\displaystyle
{E}_{\pi_\theta}
\left[
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{arr}}|}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{arr}}}
\Delta_i
\right],
\end{array}
\end{equation}
over the arrived vehicles \(\mathcal{N}^{\mathrm{arr}}\).  The per-step
rewards \(r\) are instantiated in Section~\ref{sec:method}.

\subsection{World-Model Formulation}
\label{sec:wm-formulation}

\(\mathcal{T}\) is accessible only through microscopic simulation, and the
network-wide effect of a route choice emerges only after many epochs, so
direct policy optimization on \(\mathcal{M}\) is costly.  \(\mathcal{T}\)
couples two processes---network-wide traffic evolution and per-vehicle
route progress---which we model at two levels, a learned macroscopic
transition and an analytic microscopic advancement, composed into imagined
rollouts for policy learning.

\paragraph{Macroscopic traffic level.}
The state is the segment traffic field \(X_t=\chi(s_t,G)\),
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{l}
X_t(u)=
[\mathrm{c}_t(u),\ v_t(u),
\kappa(u),
\ell(u),\ v_{\max}(u)],
\end{array}
\label{eq:traffic-field}
\end{equation}
with vehicle count \(\mathrm{c}_t\), speed \(v_t\), capacity \(\kappa\),
length \(\ell\), and speed limit \(v_{\max}\).  \(X_t\) is a lossy
segment-aligned view of \(s_t\); the removed identities, destinations, and
route suffixes re-enter through the microscopic level.  The action is the
planned CAV pressure \(\Psi_t^{\mathrm{CAV}}\), the projection of the joint
route choices onto the segment graph, describing where the controlled
vehicles intend to travel.  With \(Z_t=f_\phi(X_t,G)\), the learned
transition \(F_\phi\), both neural networks (Section~\ref{sec:method}),
predicts
\begin{equation}
  \begin{array}{rcl}
  \hat Z_{t+1}
  &=&
  F_\phi(
  Z_t; \Psi_t^{\mathrm{CAV}},D_t,H_t^{\mathrm{HDV}}
  ).
  \end{array}
  \label{eq:macro-transition}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Microscopic vehicle level.}
Each active CAV is tracked by its observation \(o_{i,t}\), current segment,
route suffix, and arrival status, and acts by \(a_{i,t}\).  A deterministic
wrapper advances it within one decision interval:
\begin{equation}
(u_{i,t+1}, P^{\mathrm{rem}}_{i,t+1})
=
\mathrm{Advance}\!\big(u_{i,t}, P^{a_{i,t}}_{i,t};
\widehat\tau_{t+1}, \Delta t\big),
\qquad
\widehat\tau_t(u) = \frac{\ell(u)}{\widehat v_{t,u}},
\label{eq:micro-advance}
\end{equation}
with \(\widehat v_{t,u}\) decoded from the macroscopic state; departing
vehicles are added and arrived ones removed.  Both levels thus evolve from
one prediction.

\paragraph{Rewards at the two levels.}
The per-vehicle reward charges CAV \(i\) the imagined travel time
accumulated before its next decision or arrival; the global reward charges
the travel time accumulated by all present vehicles in each interval, whose
episode sum matches Definition~7.  Both are computed from decoded readouts
(Section~\ref{sec:method}).
